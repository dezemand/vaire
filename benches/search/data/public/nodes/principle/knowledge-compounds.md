# Knowledge compounds

References get reused constantly; [[concept:entity|entities]] get created seldom. That
asymmetry is what makes a growing corpus more valuable per entry over time rather than
merely bigger: every new record that links to an existing entity adds a citation the next
reader benefits from, without adding anything for a maintainer to curate.

The same idea governs what belongs inside a [[concept:package]] at all — every new entity
should either help answer one of the questions the package already claims to answer, or
justify adding a new one to that list; an entity that answers no question the package poses
sits in the wrong boundary. It's also why the human side of the
[[concept:entity-creation-pass]] stays manageable indefinitely: because identity is created
so much less often than it's referenced, what reaches a person is a trickle of genuine "new
entity, or that one?" decisions, never a flood that could fall behind.
